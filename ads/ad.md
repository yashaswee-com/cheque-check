# Ad Video Creative & Production Guide

> **Purpose:** This is the master reference document for creating new
> CHEQUE-CHECK advertising angles, scripts, and Google Flow
> video-generation prompts. When this file is supplied with a new ad
> idea, use it as the source of truth for audience, brand positioning,
> characters, environments, dialogue, visual continuity, and production
> rules.

---

## 1. How to use this document

Use this guide as the production source of truth for CHEQUE-CHECK ads.
Follow the rules in order:

1. Read the user's brief and identify the requested deliverable, target
   audience, language, creative angle, format, and any supplied script
   or reference assets.
2. Select one central customer problem and one relevant product benefit.
   Do not combine unrelated angles just to mention more features.
3. If the user requests a script, provide the script only. If the user
   requests a complete video prompt but has not supplied an approved
   script, write the script first and ask for approval before creating
   visual-generation directions. A supplied final script or the user's
   explicit approval to proceed without review satisfies this gate.
4. After the script is approved, create the requested production
   prompt. Preserve every approved dialogue line exactly.
5. If the user requests only a revision to an already approved prompt or
   script, change only the requested parts and preserve all other
   approved details.

Keep the ad conversational and relatable: it should feel like an
entertaining interaction between business owners, not a formal software
demonstration.

### Instruction priority and uncertainty

- Follow the user's current brief for the requested angle, language,
  duration, platform, and deliverable, unless it conflicts with product
  accuracy or a mandatory production constraint in this guide.
- Treat product capabilities stated in this guide as the confirmed
  baseline. A clearly stated, updated product fact from the user may
  replace that baseline; do not infer new capabilities from a creative
  idea or visual reference.
- Use the exact character and environment references in this guide
  unless the user supplies updated reference assets and asks to use
  them.
- Do not invent missing details that affect the product claim, approved
  dialogue, or story logic. Ask only the essential question needed to
  proceed. For minor creative gaps, choose the simplest option
  consistent with this guide.
- When a request conflicts with a rule, identify the conflict and ask
  for clarification rather than silently making a material change.

### Deliverable modes

- **Script only:** Return the title/angle, scene structure, estimated
  duration, and speaker-labelled dialogue. Do not include camera or
  video-generation prompts.
- **Complete video prompt:** After the approval gate, include a positive
  prompt and a separate negative prompt. Include scene-by-scene visual
  direction, dialogue-level acting, continuity, camera, audio, props,
  and UI instructions.
- **Revision:** State or show only the requested changes unless the
  user asks for the complete revised deliverable.
- Do not add explanations, alternate versions, captions, hashtags, or
  extra CTAs unless requested or needed to resolve an ambiguity.

## 2. Product overview

**Product name:** CHEQUE-CHECK

CHEQUE-CHECK is a mobile-first cheque-management assistant for Indian
business owners who regularly issue and receive cheques.

### Core product value

CHEQUE-CHECK helps business owners:

- Capture cheque information by taking a photo of a cheque.
- Extract key details such as cheque date, amount, and party name
  using AI.
- Keep cheque information organised in one place.
- Track which party a cheque belongs to and which bank account is
  relevant.
- Receive a phone reminder before a recorded cheque date, so they can
  prepare funds in the relevant account.
- Get a clearer view of upcoming cheque commitments instead of
  depending entirely on memory, diaries, or registers.

### Product positioning

Position CHEQUE-CHECK as:

**A personal cheque-management assistant for Indian business owners who
don't want to lose track of a single cheque.**

It is not to be presented as generic accounting software or a
complicated enterprise platform. Keep the promise simple: record cheque
details, know what is coming up, and get reminded in time.

### Product-claim accuracy

- It is safe to say the app extracts cheque date, amount, and party
  name from a cheque photo, as described in the product brief.
- It is safe to say the app reminds users by phone before a recorded
  cheque date.
- It is safe to say the app helps users remember which account needs
  funds, based on the cheque information they have recorded.
- Do **not** claim that CHEQUE-CHECK independently checks or verifies a user's live bank balance unless that capability is explicitly
  confirmed.
- Do **not** promise that cheque bounce will be prevented in every case.
- Do **not** imply that the app controls bank processing, supplier
  dispatch, or cheque clearance.
- Do not invent integrations, features, guarantees, or financial
  outcomes.

## 3. Target audience

### Primary audience

Traditional Indian business owners who regularly issue and receive
cheques and find it difficult to track cheque dates, amounts, parties,
and bank accounts.

### Core audience profile

- **Age hypothesis:** Approximately 35--50.
- **Geography:** Primarily Tier-2 and Tier-3 Indian business markets.
- **Business type:** Established wholesale, distribution, and trading
  businesses.
- **Cheque volume hypothesis:** Approximately 30--100+ cheques per
  month.
- **Business turnover hypothesis:** ₹2--15 crore annually. Treat this
  as a targeting hypothesis, not a hard eligibility requirement.
- **Banking setup:** Often uses multiple bank accounts.
- **Current workflow:** Diary, register, cheque book, Excel, Tally,
  WhatsApp, or a combination of manual records and tools.
- **Likely users:** Business owner and accountant/accounts manager.
- **Indicative willingness-to-pay hypothesis:** ₹299--₹999 per month,
  subject to market validation.

**Most important qualification:** Actual cheque volume and
cheque-management pain matter more than age, turnover, or industry. A
business handling many cheques every month is a stronger prospect than a
larger business that rarely uses cheques.

### Industries to test

These are segments to validate, not assumptions that every business in
the category uses cheques heavily:

1. Textile, saree, and garment wholesalers.
2. Steel and metal traders.
3. Pharmaceutical distributors.
4. FMCG distributors.
5. Construction-material suppliers.
6. Auto-parts wholesalers.
7. Chemical and plastic traders.
8. Paper and packaging businesses.
9. Transport and logistics businesses.
10. Contractors and other cheque-heavy trading businesses.

### Main customer persona: Manish

Manish represents a traditional Indian business owner, such as a saree
or garment wholesaler in a Tier-2 or Tier-3 city. He has an established
business, deals with multiple suppliers and customers, handles many
cheques, and uses a diary or register to keep track of them.

He is practical and hardworking. He is not necessarily against
technology; he simply does not want complicated software or extra work.
He responds to simple, visible benefits that solve a familiar business
problem.

### Common customer problems

Use one central problem per ad wherever possible:

- Forgetting an upcoming cheque date despite writing it in a diary.
- Having to rush to the bank at the last minute to arrange or deposit
  funds.
- Not remembering which party's cheque is due and for what amount.
- Losing track of which bank account needs funds for an upcoming
  cheque.
- Facing bank charges or supplier friction after a cheque-related
  mistake.
- Maintaining cheque information across several registers, messages,
  and accounts.
- Spending time repeatedly checking records to understand upcoming
  commitments.

Do not cram every pain point into every advertisement. Pick one and make
it vivid.

## 4. Advertising strategy

### The desired viewer reaction

The viewer should think:

**"Yeh toh meri dukaan ki problem hai!"**

The ad should make a business owner recognise their own daily experience
before introducing the app.

### Creative principles

- The first 1--3 seconds must create curiosity or recognition.
- Start with a specific situation, challenge, observation, or playful
  question.
- Prefer a natural conversation over a formal product pitch.
- Make the problem concrete: a cheque date, a party, an amount, a bank
  account, a diary, or a last-minute bank visit.
- Use familiar business situations and everyday Hindi.
- Introduce CHEQUE-CHECK only after the problem is clear.
- Demonstrate one relevant product action or benefit.
- End with a clear, simple payoff or CTA.
- The advertisement should feel like an organic Reel first and an
  advertisement second.
- Avoid generic openings such as "Do you face problems managing your
  business?"
- Avoid unnecessary technical explanations.
- Avoid overloading one ad with a list of unrelated features.
- Do not add a "share this with a friend" CTA unless the user
  explicitly requests it. Paid advertisements do not need a forced
  sharing CTA.

### Possible creative angles

Use these as starting points, not as a fixed list:

1. **Forgotten cheque date:** The owner writes it down but still
   forgets.
2. **Wrong account / funds preparation:** The owner remembers the
   cheque but not which account needs funds.
3. **Last-minute bank rush:** The owner has to arrange funds at the
   last moment.
4. **Supplier relationship:** A cheque-related issue creates friction
   with a supplier.
5. **Multiple parties:** Too many customers and suppliers to remember.
6. **Multiple businesses or accounts:** Cheque information is spread
   across different records.
7. **Accountant overload:** The accountant is repeatedly checking
   registers and reminding the owner.
8. **Traditional register versus organised records:** A relatable
   contrast, without insulting traditional business owners.
9. **Business-owner challenge:** Amit asks Manish to recall an upcoming
   cheque's party, date, or amount without checking a register.
10. **Cost of a small mistake:** A missed detail can cause bank charges,
    wasted time, or business inconvenience. Keep claims accurate and
    avoid exaggerated guarantees.

## 5. Character reference guide

Use only the exact character reference names below in prompts. Do not
create alternate aliases.

### `@Amit`

**Character identity:** Mid-30s, modern, tech-savvy Indian businessman.

**Personality and presence:**

- Confident, calm, friendly, and approachable.
- A helpful peer who understands business owners' everyday problems.
- Never arrogant, condescending, or overly salesy.
- Speaks with clarity and reassurance.
- Uses relaxed posture and natural open-hand gestures.
- May smile or lightly tease Manish in a friendly way.
- Handles the phone naturally and confidently when demonstrating the app.

**Dialogue style:**

- Conversational, clear Hindi when the ad is in Hindi.
- Uses familiar business language and addresses Manish naturally as
  "Manish bhai".
- Sounds like a business friend sharing a useful solution, not a
  voice-over artist reading a sales pitch.

**Performance rules:**

- During conversational dialogue, Amit looks at Manish.
- Amit does not look into the camera throughout the ad by default.
- Amit turns to face the viewer only when the script explicitly assigns
  him a direct-to-camera CTA.
- When a line is addressed to Manish, Amit's face and body are oriented
  towards Manish.

### `@Manish`

**Character identity:** Early-40s, traditional Indian saree/garment shop
owner.

**Personality and presence:**

- Grounded, hardworking, earnest, and relatable.
- May be slightly overwhelmed by manual cheque records.
- Never portray him as foolish or technologically incapable; he is a
  busy business owner managing many responsibilities.
- Expresses frustration naturally when describing a real business
  problem.
- Becomes curious and relieved when Amit demonstrates a useful
  solution.
- Uses a diary, register, cheque book, or other relevant prop when the
  script calls for it.

**Dialogue style:**

- Conversational Hindi, using everyday words and a natural Indian
  business-owner rhythm.
- Addresses Amit naturally as "Amit bhai".
- Avoids theatrical overacting.

**Performance rules:**

- When Manish says he writes details in a diary/register, it is visibly
  in his hands or on the counter in front of him.
- He looks at Amit while replying unless the action requires him to
  inspect a prop or phone.
- While listening, he remains silent and reacts naturally.
- He never speaks Amit's lines.

### Relationship between characters

Amit and Manish are familiar business acquaintances or friends. Their
interaction should feel warm and believable.

- Amit calls him "Manish bhai".
- Manish calls him "Amit bhai".
- They speak to each other naturally.
- Do not make their conversation sound like a formal interview or
  scripted corporate presentation.

## 6. Environment reference guide

Use only the exact environment reference names below.

### `@Shop`

**Environment identity:** Manish Saree Store, a traditional Indian saree
and apparel shop.

**Visual details:**

- Floor-to-ceiling polished wooden shelves.
- Colourful folded silk and cotton sarees.
- Hindi/English shop signboard.
- Customers or staff may appear in the background where appropriate.
- Warm tube-light and display lighting.
- Polished concrete floor.
- A shop counter/desk suitable for placing a diary, cheque, and
  smartphone.

**Usage:** - This is the primary environment for conversations between
Amit and Manish. - Keep the same shop layout, shelves, counter,
background arrangement, and lighting across scenes in one ad. - Use the
same reference in every scene prompt. - Do not replace it with a generic
office, showroom, bank, or different shop.

### `@Street`

**Environment identity:** Busy Tier-2 Indian marketplace road.

**Visual details:** - Local shoppers. - Auto-rickshaws. - Colourful
storefront signs. - Overhead wires. - Afternoon sunlight.

**Usage:** Use only when the ad angle specifically requires a
marketplace or exterior street setting.

### `@Bank`

**Environment identity:** CBI Bank public-sector branch.

**Visual details:** - Glass entrance. - Guard in brown uniform. -
Scooters and pedestrians. - Commercial area during daytime.

**Usage:** Use only when the ad angle specifically requires a bank visit
or bank exterior.

### Environment continuity rules

- Use the exact same environment reference for every scene in a single
  ad unless the script explicitly includes a location change.
- Keep time of day and lighting consistent.
- Keep props and furniture in plausible positions between cuts.
- Do not let the background change just because the camera angle
  changes.
- A close-up, medium shot, or reverse angle must still belong to the
  same physical location.
- Do not introduce an unrequested location or new speaking character.

## 7. Props and product demonstration

Use props only when they support the story.

### Diary/register

- Show a real physical diary or register when a character says he
  records cheque details manually.
- If Manish says "diary mein likhta hoon", show him holding, opening,
  or pointing to the diary.
- Keep the diary visible during the relevant dialogue.

### Cheque

- Use a realistic physical cheque placed flat on a desk or counter.
- Keep the cheque visually clear enough to understand the scanning
  action.
- Avoid showing sensitive real banking information.
- Use fictional/sample cheque details only if text is required.

### Smartphone and CHEQUE-CHECK UI

Whenever CHEQUE-CHECK appears on a phone:

- Use **only the blue-and-white theme**.
- Keep the interface minimal, clean, professional, and uncluttered.
- Make the phone screen face the camera and remain large enough to
  see.
- Show only the UI relevant to the spoken dialogue.
- Do not create a crowded dashboard.
- Do not use purple, green, orange, or unrelated colours as the app's
  main theme.
- Do not generate random or garbled text.
- If the generated text is unreliable, use a clean generic
  blue-and-white screen and composite the real app UI later.

### Approved simple scanning-screen layout

When a cheque scan is demonstrated, use this restrained layout:

**Header:** `Amit Enterprises Cheque Check`

**Main content:** A cheque-scanning screen showing these extracted
fields:

- Amount
- Date
- Party Name

**Bottom navigation:** Exactly four navigation items, in this order:

1. Home icon
2. Report icon
3. Settings icon
4. Cheque icon

Do not add other labels, cards, charts, or dashboard elements unless the
user supplies an updated UI reference.

### Physical scanning action

When the script says Amit scans a cheque:

1. Amit places the cheque flat on the desk/counter.
2. He opens the CHEQUE-CHECK app.
3. He positions the phone camera above the cheque.
4. He taps the capture/shutter button.
5. Show the photo being taken.
6. Then show the simple extracted-details screen.

Do not skip the physical action and jump straight to a result screen.

## 8. Dialogue and language rules

### Language selection

At the start of each ad brief, follow the language requested by the
user.

- If Hindi is requested, keep all dialogue and audible background
  conversation in Hindi.
- If English is requested, keep all dialogue and audible background
  conversation in English.
- Do not switch languages or add unrequested Hinglish.
- Product names and familiar business terms may remain as written in
  the approved script.

### Hindi dialogue style

- Use everyday, conversational Hindi that a traditional Indian
  business owner would naturally speak.
- Keep sentences easy to understand on first hearing.
- Common terms such as cheque, party, bank, account, diary, register,
  photo, phone, and app are acceptable where natural.
- Avoid unnecessarily complex English words such as "automated
  reminder", "centralised dashboard", "workflow", or "account-wise
  reconciliation".
- Prefer simple phrases such as "phone karke yaad dila dega".
- Do not make dialogue overly formal or literary.

### Verbatim dialogue is mandatory

Once the user approves a script:

- Copy every dialogue exactly into the video prompt.
- Do not silently improve grammar or wording.
- Do not omit small words, repetitions, names, or phrases.
- Do not replace "date" with "tareekh" or vice versa if the approved
  line specifies one.
- Add pronunciation notes separately without changing the dialogue
  text.
- If a line is too long for the requested duration, flag the timing
  issue and ask whether the user wants a longer scene or a shorter
  approved line. Do not silently cut the line.

### Speaker assignment

For every spoken line, explicitly state:

- Speaker.
- Exact dialogue.
- Who the speaker is looking at.
- Whether the other character is silent.
- Facial expression.
- Body orientation and gestures.
- Relevant prop action.
- Camera framing.

Only the assigned speaker moves their lips during that line.

## 9. Video format, scene structure, and pacing

### Default format

- **Aspect ratio:** Vertical portrait 9:16 only.
- **Style:** Photorealistic, cinematic Indian commercial
  advertisement.
- **Typical structure:** 3 scenes/cuts, unless the user requests a
  different structure.
- **Default maximum duration:** 15 seconds per scene, unless the user
  specifies otherwise.
- **Total duration:** Determined by the number and length of scenes
  requested.

### Scene structure

A common three-part structure is:

1. **Problem:** Hook and customer pain.
2. **Solution:** Introduce CHEQUE-CHECK and demonstrate one relevant
   feature.
3. **Payoff and CTA:** Explain the practical benefit and invite the
   viewer to act.

This structure is flexible. Choose the structure that best serves the
new creative angle.

### Timing rules

- Allocate time according to the actual number of words and natural
  speaking pace.
- Do not force long dialogue into a short duration.
- Allow enough time for pauses, reactions, prop actions, and phone
  demonstrations.
- If a line needs 12--14 seconds, allocate 12--14 seconds to it.
- Do not speed up speech to satisfy an arbitrary timing target.
- Never omit words to fit the clip.
- Keep every scene within the user's specified maximum duration.

### Continuity between cuts

- Treat scenes as consecutive shots from one filming session.
- Preserve character positions where practical.
- Keep props consistent. A diary put on the counter should not
  suddenly reappear in a character's hand without an action.
- Keep the smartphone in the same character's possession unless the
  script shows it being handed over.
- Preserve lighting, shop layout, background extras, and time of day.
- Use natural shot-reverse-shot editing rather than unrelated
  disconnected clips.

## 10. Dialogue-level visual prompt format

When writing a Google Flow prompt, visual directions must be attached to
each dialogue, not only described generally at the beginning of a scene.

For every dialogue, include:

1. **Duration:** Estimated seconds for the line.
2. **Speaker:** Exact character reference.
3. **Exact dialogue:** Verbatim approved text.
4. **Camera:** Shot type, framing, and camera position.
5. **Orientation:** Who the speaker is facing.
6. **Action:** What the character is doing while speaking.
7. **Expression:** Facial expression and emotional delivery.
8. **Other character:** What the listener is doing; explicitly state
   that they remain silent.
9. **Environment:** Relevant background and continuity details.
10. **Props/UI:** Diary, cheque, phone, or app-screen instructions where
    applicable.
11. **Audio:** Voice, pronunciation, ambience, and lip-sync
    requirements.

Do not put all visual directions in one generic paragraph and expect the
model to infer which action belongs to which line.

## 11. CTA and camera-facing rules

Amit should normally speak to Manish during their conversation.

If the script includes a direct-to-viewer CTA:

1. Amit first completes the dialogue addressed to Manish.
2. Only after that line ends, Amit naturally turns his head and upper
   body towards the camera.
3. The camera reframes Amit in a medium close-up.
4. Amit maintains direct eye contact with the lens.
5. Amit delivers the CTA to the viewer, not to Manish.
6. Manish remains silent in the background and does not move his lips.
7. Keep the phone visible if it supports the CTA.

Do not make Amit face the camera for the entire final scene merely
because the scene contains a CTA. The camera-facing turn should happen
only at the CTA transition.

## 12. Audio and performance requirements

- Natural Indian voices, not robotic or overly theatrical.
- Clear Hindi pronunciation.
- Accurate lip synchronisation.
- Same voice identity for each character across every scene.
- No voice switching.
- No dialogue overlap unless specifically requested.
- No extra narration or voice-over.
- Background shop ambience should be subtle.
- Background music, if used, must remain below dialogue volume.
- Do not let sound effects cover words.
- Prioritise complete, intelligible dialogue over visual effects.

## 13. Positive and negative video prompts

For a complete video prompt, provide separate, clearly labelled
**Positive prompt** and **Negative prompt** sections. The positive prompt
describes what should appear and happen. The negative prompt lists
specific elements or errors to avoid; it must not replace required
positive direction. If the video tool has a dedicated negative-prompt
field, put the negative section there. Otherwise, include it as a
separate final section in the paste-ready prompt.

### Required final prompt layout

Use this order so the prompt is easy to review and paste into a video
tool:

1. **Positive prompt**
   - Global format, style, language, character references, and
     environment references.
   - Scene-by-scene direction in story order. Include duration and
     dialogue-level details for every spoken line.
   - Shared continuity, audio, product UI, and CTA instructions where
     relevant.
2. **Negative prompt**
   - A concise, consolidated list of applicable exclusions from the
     rules below.

Do not return only a checklist of requirements when the user asks for a
complete prompt. Write the actual scene-specific instructions, using
the checklist below to make them complete.

### Positive prompt checklist

Include only details relevant to the approved script, but make the
required direction explicit:

- Vertical 9:16, photorealistic cinematic Indian commercial style.
- Exact character and environment references, with consistent identity,
  wardrobe, location, lighting, and voice across cuts.
- Scene order, approximate duration, and the intended emotional
  progression.
- Each approved dialogue line verbatim, assigned to its speaker, with
  framing, gaze, action, expression, listener reaction, props, and
  pronunciation/lip-sync direction.
- Concrete continuity instructions for character positions, props,
  background, and time of day.
- The relevant CHEQUE-CHECK action and restrained blue-and-white UI
  only when required by the script.
- Natural ambience and subtle music, if appropriate; dialogue remains
  clear and intelligible.
- A direct-to-camera CTA only when it is present in the approved script,
  with the turn to camera occurring at the CTA transition.

### Negative prompt

Include the following applicable exclusions in the negative prompt.
Keep exclusions concise and do not add items that contradict the
approved brief:

- Change character identity, face, age, hairstyle, clothing, or voice
  between scenes.
- Change the shop environment between cuts.
- Swap dialogues between Amit and Manish.
- Make a silent character move their lips.
- Omit, paraphrase, shorten, or invent dialogue.
- Add extra speaking characters or narration.
- Add an unrequested logo intro.
- Add subtitles unless requested.
- Use landscape 16:9 framing.
- Use a cluttered or unrelated mobile interface.
- Use non-blue-and-white colours as the CHEQUE-CHECK app theme.
- Show random, distorted, or gibberish UI text.
- Show a phone screen too small or angled away from the camera when
  the UI is important.
- Skip a physical action that the dialogue says is happening.
- Make Amit address the camera during dialogue intended for Manish.
- Make Amit address Manish during a CTA explicitly intended for the
  viewer.
- Add a forced sharing CTA unless requested.
- Make unsupported claims about live bank balance verification,
  guaranteed prevention of cheque bounce, or guaranteed supplier
  outcomes.
- Show real cheque numbers, account numbers, signatures, customer
  information, or other sensitive banking details.
- Add text, logos, product features, UI labels, or claims that the user
  has not supplied or approved.
- Use distorted hands, duplicated fingers, unreadable phone UI, or
  implausible phone/cheque interactions when these are visible.
- Use abrupt location, time-of-day, lighting, wardrobe, prop, or
  character changes between consecutive shots.
- Add dramatic panic, humiliation, fearmongering, or disrespectful
  portrayals of traditional business owners.
- Add visual effects, transitions, or camera movements that distract
  from the dialogue or make the product action unclear.

## 14. Recommended workflow for each new ad

Use this workflow every time a new angle is proposed.

### Phase A - Understand the angle

Identify:

- Target business type/persona.
- The one problem the ad is about.
- The moment or situation that makes the problem relatable.
- The product feature that solves that problem.
- The desired viewer action.

Ask only for essential missing information.

### Phase B - Write the script

Provide:

- Ad title/angle.
- Scene/cut structure.
- Approximate duration per scene.
- Speaker-labelled dialogue.
- CTA, if requested.

At this stage, do not add visual-generation prompts unless the user asks
for them.

### Phase C - Finalise the script

Incorporate feedback exactly. Preserve the user's chosen lines and
wording. Do not change approved dialogue without permission.

### Phase D - Create the Google Flow prompt

After approval, create a complete prompt containing:

- Global character and environment references.
- Scene-by-scene continuity instructions.
- Dialogue-level camera, action, expression, movement, and prop
  directions.
- Exact speaker assignment.
- Audio and pronunciation rules.
- Phone UI instructions where relevant.
- CTA orientation and camera direction.
- Separate positive and negative prompt sections.

### Phase E - Production check

Before returning the final prompt, verify:

- Every approved line appears exactly once.
- Every line is assigned to the correct speaker.
- Scene durations are realistic and within the requested maximum.
- Props appear when referenced in dialogue.
- Background and character continuity are explicit.
- Phone UI matches the blue-and-white rules.
- CTA camera orientation is correct.
- No unsupported product claims have been introduced.

## 15. Final creative standard

Every CHEQUE-CHECK advertisement should satisfy these principles:

**Relatable:** The business owner recognises the situation immediately.

**Specific:** The problem involves a real cheque, date, amount, party,
account, diary, or business consequence.

**Conversational:** Amit and Manish sound like two people who know each
other, not actors reading a corporate script.

**Simple:** The audience understands the product without technical
explanation.

**Credible:** Product claims stay within confirmed capabilities.

**Visually continuous:** Characters, shop, props, lighting, and voices
remain consistent across cuts.

**Accurate:** Approved dialogue is spoken verbatim by the correct
speaker.

**Action-oriented:** The viewer understands what CHEQUE-CHECK does and
what to do next.

The ultimate test:

> Would a traditional business owner handling dozens of cheques every
> month recognise this as a problem from their own business and
> understand how CHEQUE-CHECK can help?
