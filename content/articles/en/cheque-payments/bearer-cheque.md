# Bearer Cheque: Meaning, Use and Safety

A **bearer cheque** is an open instrument payable in a manner that permits presentation by the bearer, subject to the cheque’s wording, crossings, banking rules, and current bank practice.

Because a bearer instrument can be sensitive to possession, correct storage and handover matter.

## Bearer cheque versus account-payee cheque

| Point              | Bearer cheque                                           | Account-payee cheque                                |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Intended recipient | Possessor may be relevant to encashment or presentation | Named payee through account handling                |
| Loss risk          | Higher where possession is enough for misuse            | Reduced by named-account requirements               |
| Traceability       | Payment trail depends on bank and transaction evidence  | Payee account provides clearer destination evidence |
| Common use         | Limited or tightly controlled                           | Common for business payments                        |

Actual bank handling can depend on the printed instrument, crossing, endorsement, and payment amount. Confirm with the issuing bank.

## Is a bearer cheque safe?

A bearer cheque should be treated like a valuable payment instrument:

- write it only when ready to hand over;
- record its number, date, amount, and recipient;
- avoid leaving it signed and blank;
- hand it directly to the intended recipient;
- keep proof of delivery;
- report loss quickly to the bank;
- use account-payee wording where that fits your risk and banking workflow.

## Who is the payee?

The named payee is still relevant when a cheque shows a person or business. Understand [payee meaning](/en/articles/payee-meaning) before filling an instrument.

## When businesses avoid bearer cheques

Many businesses prefer account-payee instruments because they create a more controlled destination. This supports:

- clearer reconciliation;
- better evidence;
- lower handover risk;
- easier confirmation of the intended recipient.

The right form depends on the bank’s current rules and the transaction. Ask the bank if a particular cheque can and should be treated as bearer.
