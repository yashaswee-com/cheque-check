# RTGS Cheque Format: How RTGS Forms and Cheques Differ

People often search for **RTGS cheque format**, but RTGS is a bank transfer method rather than a special type of cheque. A cheque carries payment instructions on paper; an RTGS instruction sends funds electronically between bank accounts.

## RTGS versus cheque

| Point                | Cheque                            | RTGS                                      |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Instrument           | Paper payment instruction         | Electronic fund transfer                  |
| Beneficiary identity | Payee name written on cheque      | Beneficiary name and bank account details |
| Settlement           | Depends on cheque clearing        | Processed through the RTGS system         |
| Common input         | Date, amount, payee and signature | Account, IFSC, amount and authorization   |

Your bank may use a physical cheque as debit authorization for some branch workflows, but that does not turn RTGS into a cheque format.

## Common details on an RTGS request

Banks may ask for:

- remitter account number;
- beneficiary name;
- beneficiary account number;
- beneficiary bank and branch;
- IFSC;
- transfer amount;
- transfer purpose or remarks;
- mobile number;
- account-holder authorization;
- cheque number if the bank requires debit support.

Exact forms, cut-off times, limits, fees, and acceptance rules vary by bank. Use the bank’s current RTGS form rather than an informal template.

## Filling the supporting cheque

If your bank asks for a cheque for an RTGS instruction, follow its branch guidance. Commonly, the cheque may be:

1. drawn for the transfer amount;
2. made payable as directed by the bank;
3. signed by an authorized account holder;
4. marked with the purpose requested by the bank.

Do not guess the beneficiary wording. A mismatched cheque can delay or reject the request.

## RTGS cheque format safety checklist

Before signing:

- compare account and IFSC details with trusted beneficiary information;
- check the amount in numbers and words on any cheque;
- confirm the bank’s required payee wording;
- ask about applicable fees and daily cut-off;
- retain the acknowledgement or reference number;
- never share banking passwords or OTPs to “complete” the transfer.

## When a cheque is the right choice

Use a cheque when the intended workflow is paper-based payment or scheduled deposit. Use the bank’s RTGS channel when the goal is an electronic, account-to-account transfer. Keep the references separately in your records so a future reconciliation does not confuse the two.
