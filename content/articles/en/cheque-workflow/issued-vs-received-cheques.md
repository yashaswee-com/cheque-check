# Issued vs Received Cheques: A Practical Business Workflow

An **issued cheque** is a cheque your business gives to another party to make a payment. A **received cheque** is a cheque another party gives to your business. Recording both directions correctly gives a clearer view of upcoming payments, expected collections, and the action needed for each cheque.

## The simplest difference

| Point          | Issued cheque                                       | Received cheque                                    |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Direction      | Your business pays another party                    | Another party pays your business                   |
| Main party     | Supplier, vendor, contractor, or lender             | Customer or debtor                                 |
| Main follow-up | Arrange sufficient funds before presentation        | Deposit or collect the cheque by the expected date |
| Typical status | Recorded, delivered, presented, cleared, or bounced | Received, deposited, cleared, returned, or bounced |

The labels matter because the next action is different. A payment reminder for an issued cheque should happen before funds are required. A collection reminder for a received cheque should happen before deposit or follow-up with the payer.

## Details to record for both cheque types

Keep one consistent record so a team member can understand the cheque without searching through messages or physical files:

1. Cheque number
2. Cheque date
3. Amount
4. Party name
5. Bank name
6. Business associated with the transaction
7. Bank account associated with the transaction
8. Current status
9. Expected deposit or presentation date
10. Notes and the next follow-up action

Cheque Check also recommends keeping the cheque photo or supporting record only when your business policy permits it. Always review any automatically extracted details before saving.

## A practical issued-cheque workflow

Use this flow when your business gives a cheque:

1. **Record it immediately.** Add the cheque details when the cheque is issued, not when it becomes urgent.
2. **Identify the source account.** Record which business bank account will fund the payment.
3. **Set the expected presentation date.** Work from the date the cheque is expected to be presented or deposited.
4. **Review upcoming amounts by account.** Check whether multiple cheques are due against the same account in a short period.
5. **Arrange funds in advance.** A reminder helps with timing, but the business remains responsible for arranging funds.
6. **Update the outcome.** Mark the cheque as cleared, cancelled, returned, or bounced based on the bank or party’s confirmed outcome.

This workflow is especially useful when supplier payments are spread across several bank accounts or businesses.

## A practical received-cheque workflow

Use this flow when your business receives a cheque from a customer or payer:

1. **Record the payer and cheque details.**
2. **Check the cheque date.** Post-dated cheques should remain visible before their deposit date.
3. **Assign the expected deposit or collection date.**
4. **Set a reminder.** The reminder should give enough time to locate, verify, and deposit the cheque.
5. **Track the collection outcome.** Update the record after the cheque is deposited and the bank result is known.
6. **Follow up when required.** Keep the original record visible if the cheque is returned or bounced.

A received-cheque record is not a bank-clearance confirmation. The status should reflect the information your team has verified.

## Common mistakes to avoid

### Mixing the party and the bank

The party is the person or business behind the payment. The bank is the institution printed on the cheque or holding the associated account. Store both fields separately.

### Using one payment-date field for every status

A cheque can have different meaningful dates: issue date, cheque date, deposit date, and clearance date. Keep the fields distinct when available and record the next action in notes.

### Forgetting which business owns the cheque

If you operate multiple businesses, associate each cheque with the correct business and account. This avoids totals from one business being used to plan another.

### Treating reminders as payment confirmation

A phone call, push notification, or diary reminder is only a prompt. It does not prove that funds were arranged, a cheque was deposited, or the bank cleared it.

## A useful weekly review

Once a week, review:

- Cheques due in the next seven days
- Issued cheques grouped by bank account
- Received cheques waiting for deposit
- Cheques with an overdue status
- Returned or bounced cheques requiring follow-up
- Missing party, account, or cheque details

This short review gives your team a better picture than searching through separate diaries, messages, and spreadsheets.
