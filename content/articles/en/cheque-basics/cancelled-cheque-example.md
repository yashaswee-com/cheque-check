# Cancelled Cheque Example and Safe Preparation Guide

A useful **cancelled cheque example** has the bank’s printed account details visible, a clear cancellation marking across the centre, and no payment-ready amount.

The actual appearance depends on the bank and cheque design, but the safe preparation principles are consistent.

## What the example should show

Keep these fields readable:

- printed account-holder name;
- bank name and branch;
- account number;
- IFSC;
- MICR number;
- bank logo and cheque format.

Leave this visual structure intact so the recipient can verify the banking information.

## What to write or draw

1. Write **CANCELLED** in clear capital letters across the centre.
2. Draw two parallel lines across the cheque.
3. If you must sign, follow the requester’s written instruction.
4. Keep the amount field and payee area blank unless the organization explicitly requests otherwise.

Do not make a visual sample by altering a cheque that will later be issued.

## Simple cancelled cheque example

```text
Account holder: [printed account name]
Account number: [leave readable]
Bank / branch: [leave readable]
IFSC / MICR: [leave readable]

Across centre: CANCELLED
Lines: Two parallel lines across the cheque
Amount: Blank
Payee: Blank
Signature: Only if specifically required
```

This is an explanatory layout, not a replacement for the recipient’s checklist.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Covering account or IFSC details
- Writing a payment amount
- Naming a payee
- Signing multiple blank areas
- Sending an uncancelled blank cheque
- Using an editable copy to disguise changed details

## Before sharing

Confirm who requested the cheque and why. Use the official secure upload or document channel. A cancelled cheque carries sensitive banking identifiers, so share the minimum required copy.
