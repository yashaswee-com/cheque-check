# PDC Cheque Means What?

**PDC cheque means post-dated cheque.** It is a cheque written with a future date. The date tells the holder when the cheque becomes available for the intended presentation or deposit.

For example, if a business issues a cheque on 5 March but writes 5 April as the cheque date, it is a PDC cheque.

## Why businesses use PDC cheques

Businesses may use post-dated cheques for:

- instalment or staged supplier payments;
- rent and lease schedules;
- planned customer collections;
- advance payment arrangements;
- repayment schedules agreed with a party.

A PDC is not a bank clearance guarantee. The account must still have funds, and the bank must process the cheque when it is presented.

## Is PDC cheque the same as a normal cheque?

The instrument is still a cheque. “PDC” describes the date relationship:

| Cheque type             | Meaning                                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Current-dated cheque    | The cheque date is the date of issue or earlier                           |
| Post-dated cheque (PDC) | The cheque date is in the future                                          |
| Stale cheque            | The cheque is no longer valid because its presentation period has expired |

Banks may return or handle an instrument differently based on its condition, date, and applicable rules. Confirm acceptance and timing with your bank.

## What should a PDC record contain?

Track:

1. cheque number;
2. cheque date;
3. issue or receipt date;
4. amount;
5. payer or payee;
6. bank account;
7. expected deposit date;
8. status and follow-up owner.

The cheque date and the planned deposit date can differ. Keep both visible in your workflow.

## PDC tracking checklist

For an **issued PDC**, arrange funds before the planned presentation date. For a **received PDC**, store it securely and remind the responsible person before deposit.

Review the list weekly and group upcoming cheques by bank account. This helps avoid a last-minute rush caused by several PDCs becoming due together.

## Common PDC mistakes

- Forgetting the cheque because the written date is months away
- Recording only the issue date
- Mixing issued and received PDCs in one undifferentiated list
- Assuming a PDC guarantees future payment
- Depositing before the applicable date

Keep PDC records separate from ordinary diary reminders and update the outcome after bank processing.
